FILL YOUR MENUBAR / 2.0.8

Bring your menubar back alive.

Adaptive color, floating pills and a little music at the top of your Mac.

Install & start

  1. Open Fill-Your-Menubar-2.0.8-universal.dmg.
  2. Drag Fill Your Menubar to Applications, then open it there.
  3. Turn on Enable Fill Your Menubar in the lower-left corner of Settings.
  4. Choose Live for colors that follow the window beneath the menu bar. Use Allow… to grant Screen Recording, then quit and reopen the app if macOS requests it.

This build is locally ad-hoc signed, not Apple-notarized. If Gatekeeper blocks it, use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway after attempting to open the app. A managed Mac may require administrator approval. Do not disable Gatekeeper.

Requires macOS 26 Tahoe or later. The universal app includes Apple silicon and Intel executables. Open the menu bar icon at any time to change styles, switch Fill Your Menubar off or return to Settings.

Opacity changes the painted color layer. Smooth Colors eases between captured colors. Blur samples a taller strip and softens it behind the color layer; reduce opacity to reveal it. Light Reflection appears near the menu bar and fades out as the pointer moves away. Enable Borrow the screen color to match the local color beneath the pointer, such as a red window button. Inside the menu bar, it borrows the nearest screen color below the bar. Holding the background color on hover does not freeze the reflection.

Full Bar spans the screen. Floating Pills wrap the left menus and right status area. Accessibility improves sizing; inaccessible items use the fallback widths in Appearance. Some third-party status items do not expose geometry.

Permissions

  • Screen Recording: needed to preserve icons while replacing an opaque fullscreen background, and for sampled colors, sampled reflection, blur and system audio. macOS may label the setting “Screen & System Audio Recording.” Foreground preservation reads only the menu row at display resolution. Color sampling reads a 16-point strip below it; blur uses 40 points. Sampled reflection extends that strip to 80 points so it can follow nearby controls. All frames stay in memory.
  • Accessibility: needed for automatic pill geometry and Fill Window. The app reads menu/window geometry and fullscreen state. Fill Window intercepts verified green-button clicks and sets the window's position and size through Accessibility. It does not read window titles or typed text.
  • Automation: needed only when you enable “Connect Music & Spotify.” macOS asks separately for each running player.

Solid, Palette and Hue drift work without Screen Recording on the normal transparent desktop bar when sampled effects are off. Replacing an opaque fullscreen background needs permission for every style. Auto Color, Live and sample-following Fade wait for a real sample before showing. Accessibility and Automation are needed only for their respective features. The first launch starts disabled so you choose when to activate it.

Music

The spectrum visualizer analyzes system output audio locally, including browser and game audio. Choose bars or a wave, adjust sensitivity and height, and use a solid color, rainbow, palette or artwork color. A Hann-windowed FFT produces logarithmic frequency bands.

Enable Connect Music & Spotify for metadata and playback progress. Open one of those players and approve Automation. If both play, Music takes priority. Hold Option and click or drag the top 6 pixels of the display to seek. The line hides after a configurable pause.

Progress and seeking support Music and Spotify only. Browser Now Playing metadata is not integrated. Artwork colors come from local Music artwork; Spotify uses the accent fallback, because fetching Spotify cover images would require network access. The visualizer still responds to audio from all apps.

Profiles & exclusions

In App Rules, add an application and edit its appearance. Each profile saves color source, palette, shape, fade, blur, reflection and animation. Profiles switch with the frontmost app. The Settings window keeps the last external app's profile active. Use the header menu to choose which profile you are editing. Corners, music and shortcuts are global.

Excluded apps hide the overlay and stop capture while active. Their windows are also excluded from other screen streams. If a protected video service blocks playback, turn Enable Fill Your Menubar off; some services reject any screen-capture session, even one confined to a tiny strip.

Fill Window

Enable this in Effects and grant Accessibility. Clicking a standard green button fills the display's usable area without a fullscreen Space. Click again to restore its previous size. Hold Option to use the button's normal behavior. Custom controls, non-resizable windows and apps with unusual minimum sizes may not support filling. This implementation uses public Accessibility window resizing, rather than relying on localized Window → Fill menu labels.

Performance & displays

Sampling defaults to 15 fps and is configurable from 10–120 fps. This is an upper bound: ScreenCaptureKit skips unchanged frames, and Fill Your Menubar deduplicates pixel data. Generated colors need no background sampling; fullscreen foreground preservation uses a separate stream at up to 60 fps. Hover holds color processing, then resumes with a fresh sample; the stream stays open and audio can continue. Display sleep, session inactivity, exclusions and the master switch stop capture.

All Displays creates one overlay per connected screen. Audio is captured once. Actual rendering speed depends on display refresh rate and system load. Unlike the reference app's reported 60-second idle teardown, this build retains an idle stream while live mode is active and relies on frame skipping.

Troubleshooting

  • Colors do not change: grant Screen Recording, restart Fill Your Menubar, then use General → Retry Capture. Move the pointer away from the menu bar. Confirm the current app is not excluded.
  • Overlay seems faint or invisible: check that Enable Fill Your Menubar is on and that Only in full screen matches your current window. For sampled styles, confirm Screen Recording is allowed. The tint follows the native bar and hides while it auto-hides or moves between Spaces. For an opaque fullscreen bar, allow Screen Recording and retry capture. If the native background is not flat enough to separate safely, the app keeps the original controls and background visible.
  • Menu text has low contrast: In fullscreen, the app adjusts neutral text to the rendered background while preserving colored icon interiors. Outside that mode, macOS controls contrast. Choose a lighter or darker palette if its native appearance gives poor contrast.
  • Pills do not fit: allow Accessibility and adjust fallback widths. Some menu extras do not expose positions.
  • Playback is blocked: turn Fill Your Menubar off to stop capture completely. Keep it off while watching protected video.
  • No track metadata: open Music or Spotify, enable the connection, and approve Automation. Use Reconnect Players after changing permission.
  • Login launch does not work: install the app in Applications, then enable Open at Login and approve Login Items if requested.

Your data

No analytics, accounts, network requests, crash uploads or recording files. Captured frames and audio remain in memory. Preferences use the local domain local.prismbar.app. Exported settings contain your app rules and selected colors. Diagnostics export only technical status, not images, audio, titles or track names. Exports are written only to locations you choose.

To uninstall, turn off Open at Login, quit Fill Your Menubar, and remove the app from Applications. You can revoke permissions in System Settings. Source code is included in the project under the MIT license.
